# Motorsport hero artwork

Current asset: `frontend/public/pitwall-racing-hero.png`.

Created with the built-in OpenAI image generation tool for PitWall's consumer
review pass. It depicts a fictional overhead motorsport scene in an editorial art
style. It is not a photograph, an official Formula 1 asset, a team's car, a circuit
map, or evidence about the current event. No third-party attribution was removed.

At the user's request there is no caption on the hero. The image is decorative,
with empty alt text and an aria-hidden container. Event names, dates and round
numbers remain independent provider data. This document retains the actual
provenance. Next Image provides responsive optimization and preloading. A dark
surface preserves readable information if the image fails. The image does not
animate. The artwork spans the page title and event information as one continuous
background on desktop and mobile, fading into the neutral data area. The ranking
view retains a compact event header to prioritize data.

## Final generation prompt

> Use case: illustration. Asset: exceptionally refined motorsport editorial website hero, wide landscape 1792x1024. Create a beautiful art-directed overhead racing scene, a single small vivid vermilion open-wheel racing car flowing around an elegant sweeping curve, placed in the RIGHT THIRD of frame. The asphalt is deep charcoal, tactile fine film grain, not flat vector. Sweeping ivory and vermilion painted kerb lines arc elegantly from lower middle toward upper right, with long warm late-afternoon shadows and subtle copper light. Quiet expansive near-black asphalt negative space across the entire LEFT HALF for white editorial typography. Composition like a premium motorsport magazine cover without any text, sophisticated architectural geometry, graphic beauty, restrained colors, analog photographic texture interpreted as editorial art, polished and evocative. The car is seen directly overhead, recognizable simple coherent open-wheel silhouette with four tyres and wings, small relative to the scene, with no markings or sponsors. No photorealistic garage, no showroom, no neon, no glow, no smoke, no logos, no numbers, no words, no watermark, no fake telemetry, no specific identifiable real circuit. Prioritize the beautiful curve and negative space rather than a giant car render. Warm ivory, burnt vermilion and midnight charcoal only. This is decorative motorsport artwork, not a depiction of an actual event.
